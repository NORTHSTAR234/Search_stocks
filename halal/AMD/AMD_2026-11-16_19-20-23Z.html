A. Why is AMD falling while its AI sales are accelerating?

Why the price moved

The central puzzle: investors are repricing regulatory, valuation and cash-conversion risk even though AMD is reporting sharp revenue and earnings growth.

Measured move: AMD consolidated 12→13 Nov close $585.30→$534.06 (−8.75%). Like-for-like Alpaca IEX-only AMD −8.80% vs QQQ −1.19%, a −7.61 percentage-point divergence. Different consolidated/IEX populations are labelled separately.

↘
New export restrictions threaten advanced AI-chip sales

On 16 November, US rules tightened access to advanced chips and remote AI computing through several third countries, including Southeast Asian markets. This is a confirmed regulatory development, but AMD-specific lost revenue has not been quantified; the market reaction is a plausible exposure-risk repricing.

The whole semiconductor sector is being repriced

AMD lost 8.75% at its 13 November consolidated close. Over the same preceding IEX sessions AMD fell about 8.80% while Nasdaq-100 tracker QQQ fell 1.19%; the 7.61 percentage-point gap indicates stock-specific amplification, not proof of any one cause. On 16 November, chip-sector selling continued.

A rapid previous rally increased profit-taking vulnerability

AMD rose from $404.40 at 5 November's consolidated close to $585.30 on 12 November—about 44.7% in five trading sessions. Such a rapid re-rating left the stock sensitive to risk-off headlines. Profit-taking is plausible but individual trading motives are not established.

FACT + PLAUSIBLE LINK · AMD IR · 5–12 Nov
◴
Growth and accounting profit exceed recent cash generation

The 3 November release showed 45% revenue growth and 97% Data Center growth in Q3, but free cash flow was only $554m against GAAP net income of $2.428bn; Q3 inventory rose to $11.463bn. A high valuation with weak near-term cash conversion increases the downside if future results disappoint; no direct one-day causal linkage is proven.

Counterevidence: Q3 AI business and earnings accelerated; Yahoo's FY2027 normalized EPS consensus was revised up over the prior month. A price drop alone is not evidence that AMD's business has deteriorated.
