Why the price moved

A. Why did AMD plunge even while AI-chip sales grew?

The market may be reassessing policy and cash-generation risks faster than AMD's sales have risen. Publication of accusations and falling prices do not establish wrongdoing.

⚖
New China-related allegations intensified regulatory worries.

On 19 November Reuters reported Hunterbrook Media's claims about links to Chinese counterparties under US restrictions. AMD said it complies with export-control laws, and Reuters could not independently verify the allegations. The publication coincided with selling but does not establish a breach or isolate its price effect. Reuters · 19 Nov 2026 · CONFIRMED publication / ALLEGATIONS UNVERIFIED

↘
Semiconductor selling amplified a volatile individual stock.

On 20 November broad AI and chip stocks fell, as reported by Schwab. Same-feed IEX closes show AMD ${pct(rel)} and SOXX ${pct(sector)} in the 19–20 November window. AMD lagged the sector by ${Math.abs(rel-sector).toFixed(2)} percentage points; this shows divergence rather than a proven single cause. Schwab · 20 Nov 2026 · CONFIRMED prices / PLAUSIBLE contributor

▤
AI growth has not yet translated fully into cash.

AMD Q3 revenue grew 45% and GAAP net income reached $2.428bn, yet Q3 free cash flow was $554m, versus $1.558bn in Q2. Inventory increased to $11.463bn. Investment in future demand can explain part of this but makes valuation sensitive to execution; not proof of the latest trading catalyst. AMD Q3 · 3 Nov 2026 · CONFIRMED figures / valuation inference

◆
The underlying Data Center business provides counter-evidence.

Q3 Data Center revenue surged 97% to $8.546bn and AMD projected roughly $17bn in Q4 consolidated sales. Partnerships with major cloud operators support demand opportunity, though shipment commitments and profitability are not guaranteed. A sell-off does not itself refute the operating growth.
