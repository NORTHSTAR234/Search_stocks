E. Growth opportunities

  • Value-added services: fraud prevention, cybersecurity, identity, analytics and consulting monetization grew ~19% in Q3; mix can support premium margins beyond core transaction switching.
  • Cross-border payments: Q3 local-currency cross-border volume +11%; travel and international commerce support richer unit economics, although sensitive to slowdown and fees.
  • New payments and agentic commerce: the Global Money Platform with BVNK, tokenization, real-time cross-border flows, and agentic tools could expand Mastercard's reachable payments pool; implementation and regulatory risks remain.

  • Regulation and litigation: interchange, merchant rules, antitrust and settlement matters in the US/UK/Europe remain material. The September 2026 10-Q discusses more than $5B of claimed single damages by certain opt-out US litigants and approximately $0.7B unresolved UK and Pan-European claims; claims are not judgments.
  • Premium multiple: a stable EPS path may still produce negative share-price returns if the market pays a lower multiple.
  • Macro, technology and competition: cross-border weakness, low-cost account-to-account rails, digital wallets, issuer/client concentration and cybersecurity or operational disruption may reduce growth.

Thesis invalidation: sustained loss of network volume growth, deterioration in take rates or services margins, materially adverse litigation outcomes, or EPS revision declines over multiple quarters. Even good earnings do not guarantee an immediate share-price rise.
