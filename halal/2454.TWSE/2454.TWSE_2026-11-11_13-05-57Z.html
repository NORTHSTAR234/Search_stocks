E. Growth opportunities

  • Custom AI accelerators: initial customer shipments began in 2026; earnings call described meaningful Q4 deployments and more project ramps. Incremental AI data center business could lift absolute earnings and margin, but customer forecasts are conditional.
  • Advanced smart-edge chips: AI-ready smartphones, edge compute and connectivity can increase chip complexity and device content per unit, offset by smartphone shipment cycles.
  • Wi-Fi, automotive and diversified connected devices: broader addressable markets provide growth outside handsets; fabless architecture allows innovation without owning foundry capex, but increases TSMC/supply reliance.

  • Customer/project concentration: AI ASIC ramps may rely on a small number of cloud customers and ambitious shipping schedules.
  • Cycle and competitive pressure: premium smartphone processors face competition, while AI infrastructure spending could slow; smartphone mix influences gross margin.
  • Supply chain and geopolitical risk: TSMC fabrication dependence, advanced packaging availability, Taiwan geopolitical risk, export controls, FX and cost inflation.

Thesis breaks on repeated guidance misses, unprofitable AI accelerator ramps, structural gross margin deterioration, material customer loss or sustained failure to convert accounting profits into cash. High-volatility drawdowns can precede those signals and should not be treated as earnings evidence.
