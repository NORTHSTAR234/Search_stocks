Why the price moved

A. Why has Schneider Electric fallen while sales are growing?

The core issue: Strong growth can coexist with falling shares when investors change the valuation paid for future per-share earnings, acquisition debt, integration costs and conversion of growth into free cash flow.

The PTC acquisition raises execution and valuation uncertainty.

On 5 October 2026 Schneider signed a proposed $22.6bn (€20.1bn) cash purchase of industrial-software provider PTC, with a 42.3% premium to PTC's undisturbed price. Software synergies could be valuable, but integrating an expensive business and maintaining future per-share cash flow are more difficult to price. This is a plausible multi-week valuation contributor, not proof of the 17 November daily cause.

New shares dilute the value of existing ownership.

On 20 October Schneider raised €6.0bn by placing 25m new shares at €240, around 4.32% of share capital before issuance. That provides funding but splits future earnings across additional shares; the stock also traded lower around the announcement. €240 is an issuance price, not a guaranteed floor.

Bond financing adds interest costs and leverage scrutiny.

On 28 October a €6.0bn bond was issued with an approximately 4.3% average coupon and 8.2-year average maturity. Coupons and PTC integration expenses could absorb future free cash flow even if sales continue growing. Issued gross debt should not be mechanically added to old net debt while proceeds are held as cash.

E · Competitive position and risk

E. Why the business can grow—and why the acquisition matters

Schneider has entrenched low- and medium-voltage electrical equipment, UPS, data-centre cooling, building energy management and industrial automation relationships. Installed-base integration, ecosystem and service switching costs support a durable franchise; electrification, power reliability, grid upgrades and expanding data centres provide multi-year end-market demand. Q3 Energy Management organic growth was reported near +18.8% versus Industrial Automation near +8.1% (issuer segment detail).

PTC expands potential software/data capabilities, including CAD and PLM, but the $22.6bn consideration and projected synergies make timing, integration, regulators and leverage relevant. Do not value prospective synergies as cash already earned. Deal delays, interest expenses, customer spending pauses, data-centre construction bottlenecks, FX shifts and a valuation multiple decline are material risk channels. Company-specific confirmed short-interest/social sentiment and post-deal free-cash-flow projections are NOT RATED.
