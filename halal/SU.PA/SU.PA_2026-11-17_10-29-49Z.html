On 5 October 2026, Schneider agreed to acquire PTC for about $22.6bn (€20.1bn) in cash, paying a 42.3% premium to its last undisturbed closing price. The strategic software opportunity is substantial, but financing and integration introduce risk that may reduce the premium investors pay for Schneider shares. No proof links each daily move directly to the acquisition. PTC · 5 Oct 2026 CONFIRMED DEAL / PLAUSIBLE PRICE CONTRIBUTOR

A €6 billion placement diluted existing investors.

Schneider placed 25m new ordinary shares at €240 on 20 October 2026, increasing the pre-issue share count by about 4.32%. The cash supports the PTC acquisition but ownership and potential earnings are spread across more shares, which can weigh on per-share valuation. The placement price is not guaranteed support. Schneider raised its 2026 guidance, showing resilience even as the stock repriced. Investor expectations, profit conversion and the acquisition's costs remain the essential counterchecks. E. Growth opportunities, moat and risk

Core moat: installed electrical distribution equipment, Schneider's field-proven power and cooling infrastructure, industrial automation footprint and service relationships create switching and engineering friction. Data-centre electrification and grid modernization offer multi-year demand, but project timing and customer capital-spending cycles matter.

Near-term engine: energy management Q3 organic growth approximately +18.8%, industrial automation approximately +8.1%. Data-centre solutions benefit from high-density compute and power reliability; qualitative triple-digit demand commentary should not be misread as audited sector profit growth.

PTC upside vs challenge: CAD/PLM, industrial data and AI software could deepen Schneider's solutions stack; at $22.6bn equity purchase with a very high premium, revenue synergies must translate to returns above financing and integration costs. No independently reconciled PTC 2027 post-close EPS/accretion/FCF model is available. Interest costs and potential future dilutive funding remain the main balance-sheet caveat.
