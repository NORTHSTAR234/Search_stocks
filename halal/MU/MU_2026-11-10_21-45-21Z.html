E. Growth opportunities

  • HBM for AI accelerators: high-bandwidth premium memory increases chip content per system and may improve product mix and selling prices as GPU and accelerator shipments expand.
  • Hyperscale/cloud DRAM: AI inference and larger models require memory capacity and throughput, creating demand for high-density server DRAM and stronger pricing at data-center customers.
  • Advanced packaging and leading-edge products: HBM4 and HBM4E investment, node transitions and high-density DDR5 can help Micron win strategic multiyear customer commitments, though capacity expansion is expensive.

Cloud Memory and Core Data Center units together generated $34.29B of Q4 FY2026 sales, illustrating direct data-center exposure.

Consensus current quarter revenue ~$61.83B and adjusted EPS ~$38.29; management guides $61.5B ± $1.5B revenue and $38.15 ± $1.00 adjusted EPS. Watch HBM supply allocations, DRAM/NAND pricing, FY2027 gross margin, capex and EPS revisions. The event is more than 30 days from this report.

  • Memory pricing downturn: increased capacity or weaker demand can quickly reverse margin and EPS gains even with rising AI unit shipments.
  • Customer and technology concentration: reliance on a small set of hyperscale accelerator programs and HBM packaging yields creates execution and customer-budget risk.
  • Geopolitical, export and manufacturing risks: Asia exposure, export controls and large fab investments can affect shipments, costs and compliance.

Invalidate / downgrade thesis if HBM volume commitments soften, DRAM pricing rolls over for multiple quarters, free cash flow collapses due to capex or market consensus FY2027 EPS is cut substantially. A technical stop alone should not be treated as a universal investor instruction.
