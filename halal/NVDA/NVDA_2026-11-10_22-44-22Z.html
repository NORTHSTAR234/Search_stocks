E. Growth opportunities

  • AI training and inference: demand for GPU compute clusters and networking may continue to lift data-center orders and system value per deployment.
  • Vera Rubin and networking: newer integrated platforms, optics and switches can broaden NVIDIA's content in AI factories, subject to ramp timing and customer economics.
  • Software and deployment ecosystem: CUDA libraries and related software make it harder for customers to switch, while enterprise and physical-AI deployments may extend applications beyond frontier model training.

All three drivers depend on hyperscalers receiving sufficient returns on capital and NVIDIA delivering on supply and product-roadmap commitments.

F. Risks and upcoming events

NEXT EARNINGS: 17 NOVEMBER 2026 · COMPANY-CONFIRMED, AFTER NASDAQ CLOSE. The earnings call is scheduled for 2pm Pacific time (5pm Eastern) to discuss Q3 FY2027. The market should focus on Q3 revenue vs $108B ±2% company guidance, ~74% non-GAAP gross margin, next-quarter demand, customer capex, and export-compliance developments.

  • Governance and export-control scrutiny: Reuters reported allegations and an independent board investigation into an alleged foreign-intelligence targeting of NVIDIA's CEO, plus disputed export-rule implications. Treat allegations as unproven; outcomes may influence leadership, reputation and China-policy risk.
  • AI spending concentration and valuation: a handful of major customers account for large infrastructure programs; slower monetization or capex cuts could cause multiple compression.
  • Semiconductor competition/supply: custom accelerators, AMD and deployment bottlenecks could weaken forecast pricing/margins even as overall AI demand grows.

Fundamental thesis invalidation: sustained guidance misses, material order cancellations, margin erosion and lasting legal/compliance restrictions. Given the 17 Nov catalyst, a new buyer should be cautious about entering solely because RSI is oversold.
