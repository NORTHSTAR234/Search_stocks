A · Why did Deere shares fall before Q4 earnings?

Why the price moved

The 19 November decline outpaced an already weak US equity market, while the last issuer quarter showed strong construction sales but weak large-farm-equipment demand. These facts explain investor sensitivity, not a proven single-day cause.

📉
The broader stock market fell sharply on Thursday.

DE declined 3.08% to $624.89 on 19 November while the S&P 500 lost 2.02%, a 1.06-percentage-point underperformance on the same completed regular session. 🌾
Large agricultural machinery demand remains a weak spot.

In fiscal Q3, Deere's Production & Precision Agriculture sales fell 6% year over year to $3.998 billion, even as total quarterly net sales and revenues rose 5%. Deere described pricing, shipments and farm-demand pressure in its 2026 filings. This affects the earnings expectations investors will check on 25 November; it is not proof of a 19 November order shock. Construction strength provides a meaningful counterweight.

Investors will need to see whether this stronger segment persists into FY2027; its existence is positive counter-evidence rather than a demonstrated same-day catalyst. H · Recommended action and balanced evidence

Reasons to remain constructive

Construction & Forestry Q3 sales rose 18%; Q3 net income rose 7%; Deere has a broad machinery/service installed base and a recurring quarterly dividend. Each is documented in Deere's August earnings and board notices, though none guarantees FY2027 growth.

Reasons for caution

Large-ag sales fell 6% in Q3; year-to-date profit fell 4% despite 7% revenue growth; cash flow was below prior year; fiscal earnings multiple appears elevated (~33.8–35.6× on management's FY2026 guidance). Q4 is imminent and the shareholder's loss can exceed stop levels in a gap.
