Why the price moved

A. Why did PRDO struggle despite growing earnings?

Perdoceo grew its profits faster than sales, but investors faced weak near-term earnings expectations and a market-wide decline; the exact cause of each price move is not proven.

PRDO closed 5 November at $26.55, down 11.47%, versus a 4.41% decline in same-session SPY. The earnings release was posted after 4 p.m. Eastern, so it did not cause the regular-session decline; broad market selling is a plausible contributor, not a fully established explanation. Quarterly profit improved despite modest enrollment growth.

The 5 November filing reported Q3 revenue of $215.9m, up 1.9%, and diluted GAAP EPS of $0.70 versus $0.60 a year before. Student enrollment grew only about 1.6%, so expense management contributed meaningfully; the slow top-line pace may limit how much valuation investors pay for stronger profits. E · Business strengths and growth prospects

E. What could support a recovery?

Core business: Perdoceo operates career-oriented, mainly online postsecondary degree programs across institutions including Colorado Technical University and American InterContinental University. Positive drivers include disciplined operating expenses, continuing positive earnings, modest student growth and potential South University expansion. The acquisition is expected no earlier than April 2027 and remains conditional on regulatory/accreditor approvals, not a confirmed near-term earnings event. SEC acquisition agreement Issuer quarterly release

Balanced caveat: education enrollment is sensitive to student affordability, eligibility for US federal financial aid, regulatory oversight, reputational outcomes and labor-market conditions. Future top-line growth is not guaranteed just because profits recently rose.
