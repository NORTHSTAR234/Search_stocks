A. Why did Metso fall while its orders were growing?

Why the price moved

Orders are strong, but they have not yet fully converted into revenue, profit growth or cash while the Helsinki market is selling off.

Observed window: €17.12 on Friday 13 Nov → €15.59 on Thursday 19 Nov 2026; -8.94% close-to-close across four subsequent completed sessions. No independently proven causal explanation has been identified for this exact movement.

◫
New orders are growing faster than delivered sales.

Metso’s 22 October Q3 filing showed order intake up 28% to €1,614m and sales up just 3% to €1,373m. A €3,883m backlog (+20%) is future work, not immediately earned revenue. Investors may question delivery/conversion speed, but the October figures do not prove the later November share-price fall. Metso, 22 Oct CONFIRMED FACT · causal link unproven

◉
Cash conversion and earnings weakened despite order momentum.

Q3 operating cash flow fell to €136m from €266m, and EPS from continuing operations eased to €0.15 from €0.17. Adjusted EBITA still rose 4% to €230m, so the cash/profit gap merits monitoring rather than a blanket claim of business collapse. This is a plausible source of valuation caution, not proof of the November selloff. Metso, 22 Oct CONFIRMED FACT · PLAUSIBLE CONTRIBUTOR

▤
Refinancing may sharpen attention to financing costs.

Metso issued a €300m bond on 12 November to refinance existing notes and launched tenders, with related results on 18 November. Refinancing can change interest costs and maturity exposure, while Q3 net debt/EBITDA was 1.3×. A direct trading-price response to these announcements was not established.
