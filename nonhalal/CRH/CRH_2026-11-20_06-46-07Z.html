Why the price moved

A. Why is CRH falling despite revenue growth?

Growth in building-materials sales has not kept pace with cost pressure, margin deterioration and risk to expected 2026 earnings.

▤
Sales improved, but profits and margins deteriorated.

On 29 October, CRH reported Q3 revenue of $11.4bn (+3% YoY) but net income of roughly $1.2bn (−22%), with net margin 10.4% versus 13.7% a year earlier. Cost inflation and subdued residential demand constrain earnings conversion. Confirmed issuer figures do not prove the cause of each subsequent market move. Issuer · 29 Oct 2026 CONFIRMED FACT; PLAUSIBLE CONTRIBUTOR

↘
Full-year profit expectations were reset lower.

The October 29 update cut 2026 net income guidance from $3.9–4.1bn to $3.1–3.3bn and adjusted EBITDA guidance from $8.1–8.5bn to $7.8–8.0bn. Lower expected profitability can reduce the valuation shareholders pay, but the stock subsequently had both up and down sessions. SEC earnings release · 29 Oct CONFIRMED FACT; CAUSAL MECHANISM PLAUSIBLE

▣
Arcosa integration may change debt and execution risk.

CRH completed the approximately $8.5bn Arcosa transaction on 23 October, adding US construction-material assets; its success depends on integration and future returns. September 30 net-debt figures pre-date this completion, so should not be presented as post-acquisition leverage. The same deal may eventually provide positive scale benefits. CRH · 29 Oct SEC 10-Q CONFIRMED TRANSACTION; VALUATION EFFECT UNCERTAIN

The wider equity market also sold off.

Across matched completed US sessions from 9 to 19 November, CRH moved from $97.73 to $90.54 (−7.36%) while SPY moved from $752.69 to $715.87 (−4.89%).
