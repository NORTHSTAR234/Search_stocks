Why the price moved

A. Why is Komatsu falling when its sales are growing?

Komatsu's equipment sales have grown, but margin conversion is weak and the latest selloff coincided with a steep Japanese equity-market downturn. Specific share-price causation is not established.

Same-session comparison, Thursday 19 → Friday 20 November 2026: Komatsu ¥5,821 → ¥5,578 = −4.17%; Nikkei 225 59,444.21 → 56,436.65 = −5.06%; share-minus-index difference +0.88 percentage point. Both are close-to-close Japan regular sessions. The co-movement is context, not proof of causation. Sources: Komatsu price · 20 Nov, official Nikkei index · 20 Nov.

The entire Japanese market was under heavy selling pressure.

On 20 November the Nikkei 225 closed 5.06% lower amid a reported global risk-off move following Wall Street weakness. Komatsu declined 4.17% during the same completed Tokyo session, slightly less than the benchmark. Broad-market positioning plausibly contributed, but no causal flow or separate Komatsu-specific price-moving announcement is proven.

▥
Revenue growth is not becoming equally strong profits.

Komatsu's 29 October interim filing reported first-half sales of ¥2,158.6bn, up 14.1% year on year, versus operating profit of ¥286.6bn, up just 3.5%. Operating margin narrowed to 13.3% from 14.6%; that compresses the profit earned on each yen of revenue. The margin facts are confirmed, but their direct November trading impact is not.

Recent quarterly margin and investment costs remain concerns.

In July–September, operating margin was approximately 12.1% versus 13.9% a year earlier even with 13.6% sales growth. First-half operating cash flow of ¥139.3bn less ¥130.4bn capital spending leaves only about ¥8.9bn on a simple post-capex basis. This makes earnings quality and cash conversion key valuation risks, not established day-to-day selling triggers.

Issuer Q2 financial statements · 29 Oct · CONFIRMED FACT; plausible valuation mechanism

On 29 October Komatsu raised its year-ending-March-2027 operating-profit forecast from ¥555bn to ¥585bn, net income from ¥349bn to ¥382bn and revenue from ¥4,302bn to ¥4,450bn. Stronger demand and yen depreciation contributed, providing positive counter-evidence against a wholly negative operating thesis. Better guidance does not guarantee a higher share price in a broad selloff.

E. Business quality, demand and valuation watchlist

Construction, mining and utility equipment dominates Komatsu sales. Customer replacement demand, mining-project fleets and aftermarket/service relationships add resilience, but regional mining investment, global construction cycles, dealer inventories, tariffs and yen translations can swing near-term margins. In this H1 company reporting, Americas sales rose faster than consolidated sales while some other regions were broadly flat; exchange-rate changes are a significant explanatory and future guidance risk.

Key earnings-to-price issue: the company must improve operating profit conversion after the July–September margin decline and convert inventory/receivable growth into cash. FY forecast operating margin = ¥585bn / ¥4,450bn = approximately 13.15%, leaving limited room for further erosion if sales mix or costs worsen.
