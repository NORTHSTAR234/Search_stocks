Why the price moved

A. Why is Komatsu falling while its sales are growing?

Komatsu sold more equipment but turned that growth into proportionally less operating profit, so company fundamentals offer plausible valuation pressure without proving the cause of each daily price move.

Same-session market check, 18 → 19 Nov 2026: Komatsu ¥6,010 → ¥5,821 = −3.14%; Nikkei 225 59,024.28 → 59,444.21 = +0.71%; Komatsu divergence ≈ −3.85 percentage points. Same Japanese regular close scope; different index benchmark is context rather than causal attribution. Sources: Komatsu 18–19 Nov and Nikkei 18–19 Nov.

↗
Sales have grown faster than operating earnings.

For April–September, Komatsu reported ¥2,158.6bn revenue, up 14.1%, but ¥286.6bn operating profit, up just 3.5%. Operating margin fell to 13.3% from 14.6%, so investors may question conversion of growth into operating earnings. This is a documented margin difference, not proof it caused a particular session decline.

Issuer results · 29 Oct 2026 · CONFIRMED FACT · causal attribution unverified
▥
The latest quarter shows more distinct cost pressure.

July–September sales increased 13.6% to about ¥1,115.5bn while operating income slipped 1.2% to ¥135.1bn, with a 12.1% margin versus 13.9% a year earlier. The higher cost burden could weigh on valuation even though quarterly net income rose 13.3%. The exact price impact remains unconfirmed.

Issuer quarterly tables · 29 Oct 2026 · CONFIRMED FACT · possible contributor
◷
Operating cash flow barely covers recent capital expenditure.

Six-month operating cash flow was ¥139.3bn while capex was ¥130.4bn, leaving ¥8.9bn using this simple CFO-minus-capex definition. Capex increased from ¥102.6bn a year earlier, so stronger earnings have yet to generate much discretionary cash after investment. Some company reporting defines free cash flow differently.

On 19 November, Komatsu fell 3.14% even as the Nikkei 225 rose about 0.71%; this divergence alone does not identify a cause. Japan's stock market then sold off sharply on 20 November amid global risk concerns, which plausibly added pressure to cyclical equipment names, but no verified Komatsu-specific November 19–20 catalyst was identified.

E. Business quality, demand and valuation watchlist

Construction, mining and utility equipment dominates Komatsu sales. Customer replacement demand, mining-project fleets and aftermarket/service relationships add resilience, but regional mining investment, global construction cycles, dealer inventories, tariffs and yen translations can swing near-term margins. In this H1 company reporting, Americas sales rose faster than consolidated sales while some other regions were broadly flat; exchange-rate changes are a significant explanatory and future guidance risk.

Key earnings-to-price issue: the company must improve operating profit conversion after the July–September margin decline and convert inventory/receivable growth into cash. FY forecast operating margin = ¥585bn / ¥4,450bn = approximately 13.15%, leaving limited room for further erosion if sales mix or costs worsen.

H. Balanced conclusion and independent actions

Reasons to remain constructive

  • H1 sales grew 14.1% and management raised projected FY sales and profit on 29 October, citing stronger demand and yen effects.
  • Global construction/mining equipment plus parts/service and retail-finance platforms offer a broad operating franchise.
  • FY projected EPS ¥429.63 implies 13.55× at the verified 19 Nov closing price, plus forward dividend indication of 3.26% (both estimates sensitive to profit delivery).
  • Parent-equity ratio remained 53.2% of consolidated total assets at 30 September, providing some resilience.

Reasons for caution

  • Quarterly operating margin contracted to 12.1% from about 13.9%; full H1 margin fell 1.3 points despite sales growth.
  • H1 operating cash flow of ¥139.3bn only barely exceeded ¥130.4bn in capex; cash stock declined and debt plus equipment financing need monitoring.
