Executive summary

APA Group's underlying energy-infrastructure operations are not intrinsically prohibited. However, ASX:APA is not an ordinary corporate share: it is a stapled security combining units in two registered managed investment schemes, so AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21's ordinary-share screening cannot be applied as a definitive instrument-level ruling without qualified Shari'ah review. Applying SS21's equity ratios by analogy to the FY2026 stapled group nevertheless gives a decisive warning: A$14.230 billion of drawn debt divided by an estimated 30 June 2026 market capitalisation of A$13.423 billion equals about 106.01%, far above the 30% limit. A conservative upper bound on interest-taking deposits is 12.19% and PP&E alone represents 61.93% of total assets, while the consolidated 'other interest income' line is at most 0.90% of group revenue but is not cleanly separable from finance-lease income. APA Investment Trust separately identifies itself as an investment and financing entity and earned A$5.426 million of related-party interest on inter-entity loans in FY2026, creating an additional Riba-related structural concern. Practical conclusion: do not treat APA.ASX as AAOIFI-compliant; the formal plugin class remains SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED because of the stapled-trust instrument structure.

A. Business activity

APA's operating business is Australian energy infrastructure. It owns and operates gas transmission, gas processing, compression and storage assets, electricity transmission, gas-fired power generation, wind and solar generation, batteries and related infrastructure. These operating activities are not intrinsically prohibited under the AAOIFI business screen.

The listed security itself is structurally unusual. APA Group comprises APA Infrastructure Trust and APA Investment Trust, both registered managed investment schemes; one unit in each trust is stapled together and must trade as a single ASX:APA security. APA Investment Trust's FY2026 directors' report states that its principal activities are as an investment and financing entity within APA Group. Its FY2026 accounts disclose A$5.426 million of related-party interest income and A$528.679 million of inter-entity loans receivable from a subsidiary of APA Infrastructure Trust, with interest determined by reference to market rates.

Instrument-scope issue: SS21 is written for shares of corporations. Because APA.ASX is a stapled security consisting of trust units rather than a plain ordinary share, this report cannot convert the SS21 analogy into a definitive AAOIFI instrument ruling. The formal status is therefore SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED.

D. Market-capitalisation convention

AAOIFI implementation convention — reporting-date market cap. APA reported 1,323,751,413 securities on issue at 30 June 2026 and a closing security price of A$10.14 on that date. The estimated reporting-date market capitalisation is therefore A$13.422839bn.

The SS21-analogue borrowing ratio is A$14.230bn / A$13.422839bn = 106.01%. To fall to 30% using the same debt numerator, market capitalisation would need to exceed about A$47.43bn, equivalent to roughly A$35.83 per security on the FY2026 security count.

As a sensitivity, APA traded around A$11.14–A$11.15 on 12 November 2026, implying a market value near A$14.75bn; holding the FY2026 debt numerator constant gives a ratio around 96.5%, still far above 30%. This sensitivity is illustrative only because APA has since issued EUR550m of new senior unsecured notes to refinance its March 2027 maturity, so current gross debt timing differs from the FY2026 balance sheet.

E. Tradability / asset composition

APA reported A$12.520bn of property, plant and equipment and A$20.217bn of total assets at 30 June 2026. PP&E alone therefore equals 61.93% of total assets, comfortably above the 30% mixed-asset threshold. This is a conservative test because it does not need to rely on goodwill, other intangibles or other rights to pass.

F. Purification

Purification: not exactly calculable from reliable public data. The consolidated A$27m 'other interest income' line combines ordinary interest income with finance-lease income, so it is not a verified pure prohibited-income numerator. In addition, APA Investment Trust separately earns related-party interest on inter-entity loans, and the treatment of those internal stapled-structure flows for an investor-level SS21 purification calculation requires Shari'ah review.

For scale only, if the entire consolidated A$27m line were treated as prohibited, the illustrative ceiling would be:

A$27,000,000 / 1,323,751,413 = A$0.0203966 per stapled security (about 2.04 Australian cents per security).

This is not presented as a confirmed AAOIFI purification amount. It also does not cure the borrowing-ratio failure under the SS21 ordinary-share analogy.

G. Data quality and unresolved issues

  • The FY2026 debt, security count, closing price, revenue, total assets and PP&E figures are well supported by APA's annual reporting and are high-confidence inputs.
  • The listed instrument is a stapled security comprising units in two managed investment schemes rather than an ordinary corporate share; a controlling AAOIFI rule specifically validating direct application of SS21 to this structure was not verified.
  • APA Investment Trust is explicitly an investment and financing entity and reports related-party interest and inter-entity loans, which raises a separate Riba-structure concern.
  • The exact interest-taking portion of A$1.636bn cash is not separately disclosed; however, even the all-cash upper bound remains below 30%.
  • The A$27m consolidated 'other interest income' line includes finance-lease income, so exact prohibited income and purification cannot be isolated.
  • APA issued EUR550m (A$887m equivalent) of 10-year senior unsecured notes on 6 November 2026 at a fixed swapped AUD interest rate of 6.69% to partly refinance a March 2027 maturity. This later transaction is noted but not mixed into the 30 June 2026 numerator.

H. AAOIFI guidance

The formal plugin result is SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED because APA.ASX is not an ordinary share and the stapled trust structure includes a financing entity with interest-bearing inter-entity loans. Nevertheless, an investor should not interpret that yellow classification as a near-pass: applying SS21's ordinary-share ratio logic to the consolidated FY2026 group gives a borrowing ratio of about 106%, far above the 30% limit. On that share-analogue basis APA would clearly fail. A qualified Shari'ah scholar or board would need to determine the correct instrument-level treatment of the stapled trusts, but the available evidence does not support treating APA.ASX as AAOIFI-compliant.
