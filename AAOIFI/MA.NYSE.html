Executive summary

Mastercard operates a payment technology network, and unlike card issuers does not originate cardholder loans or receive cardholder interest. Its interest-bearing debt (4.98% of reporting-date equity market value) and deliberately overinclusive cash/deposit/investment bound (2.76%) pass AAOIFI SS21 30% limits. Identified net investment income and other positive net income amount to $409m / $27,482m net revenue = 1.49%, but there is no complete gross Shariah prohibited-income maximum and credit-card facilitation needs formal Shariah qualification. If a further $11.624bn prepaid customer incentives qualified as legally enforceable market-valued rights/benefits, the conditional book proxy would be 34.34%, but this is not established. Overall SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED, not a proven ratio failure or certification. A. Business and prohibited activities

Mastercard operates a global payment technology network and sells payment-network transaction processing, cybersecurity, data/insights, authentication and other value-added solutions. For nine months ended 30 Sep 2026, the payment network generated $16,148m net revenue (58.8%) and value-added services $11,334m (41.2%). Its SEC Form 10-K states Mastercard is not a card issuer or account-holder lender, and does not determine/collect issuer interest or cardholder fees. FY2025 audited 10-K · Q3 2026 10-Q.

Material Shariah question: Some payment-network fees are generated by facilitating transactions involving conventional interest-bearing credit programmes. This does not prove Mastercard itself earns cardholder interest or that its payment-processing services are necessarily prohibited, but it is not safe to certify the entire revenue stream without a Shariah-qualified examination of programme contracts and fee sources. Conventional borrowings, deposits, debt-securities holdings, FX and interest-rate derivatives, and partner settlement guarantees also require appropriate treatment. The stated business objectives should be checked under SS21 3/4/1 before a positive overall verdict.

D. SS21 3/19 — rights and benefits tradability protocol

SS21 distinguishes ordinary business benefits/rights and tangible assets from cash or debt claims, and uses market value for the qualifying 30% test in applicable mixed-asset cases. Payment-network processing, software, licences and legally enforceable contract benefits may warrant individual treatment; the book labels alone cannot prove qualification. Denominator-adjusted revaluation break-even: Assume each incentive dollar represents a fully eligible transferable right and the $11,624m booked incentive asset is replaced with its genuine market value V, all other asset values fixed: ($8,346m + V) / ($58,149m − $11,624m + V) ≥ 30%. Solving gives V ≥ $8,016.43m (68.97% of incentive carrying value). That result requires the ENTIRE category to qualify. It is not a market valuation, independent appraisal, or evidence such contract benefits are tradable under Shariah.

If BOTH all intangibles and incentives are treated as qualifying rights and hypothetically revalued down 10% together with a matching denominator reduction, the scenario is ~32.33%; with 20% cut, ~30.18%; with 30% cut, ~27.90%. No such actual valuation is established. Network software assets could have unrecorded rights and value, but no blanket inference from Mastercard's market cap is permissible. The share-tradability conclusion remains CONDITIONAL_BOOK_PROXY_BELOW, with an above-30% alternative conditional scenario only.

F. Prohibited-income purification

Recorded $257m nine-month investment income corresponds mathematically to $257m / 872.1m period-end common shares ≈ $0.295/share if, solely for sensitivity, all investment income were prohibited. This is not an established interest amount, a complete purification factor, or a payable amount. Additional Shariah-subject fees and gross positive derivatives must be classified. The proper factor under SS21 is attributable total prohibited income divided by applicable shares at period end, multiplied by the investor's shares owned at that financial-period end; user holdings are unavailable. No complete reliable user purification is calculated.

G. Limits, integrity and confidence

  • The full asset values derive from interim GAAP/SEC accounts (not current independent market values), and the current quarterly report aggregates property/equipment with ROU benefits.
  • Do not infer that prepaid incentives represent assignable rights; fees or advance costs to partners could be consumed economic costs, not transferable property.
  • Mastercard does not itself lend to consumers, but the permissibility of network/technology services for conventional credit products is a genuine contract-level Shariah question.
  • FY2025 audited Note on other intangible assets includes capitalized software and customer relationships; it is historical context only, not a fabricated 2026 breakdown.
  • Current market cap is used as sensitivity; SS21 does not prescribe stock-price averaging in the verified stored source. The AAOIFI PDF is a 2017 published compilation; review official current revisions before certification.
