Executive summary

Tesla's vehicle and energy businesses are primarily permissible and its debt (0.66%) and interest-taking deposits (0.87%) pass AAOIFI's 30% limits. Disclosed interest income alone is 1.63% of a defined total-income measure, below 5%. However, Tesla directly underwrites conventional vehicle insurance and has financing-receivable activities whose potentially prohibited income is not fully separated. The book-value tangible-asset proxy exceeds 30%, but the required market-value test is not independently verified. The overall status is SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED; this is not an AAOIFI certification.

A. Business activity

Tesla's main revenue sources are electric vehicle sales, automotive services, energy generation and storage systems and associated software. Nine-month 2026 total reported revenue was $78.669 billion: automotive $57.310 billion, energy $8.428 billion, services/other $12.931 billion. The primary manufacturing and energy activities are not inherently prohibited under the AAOIFI screen.

Important ancillary activity: Tesla directly underwrites conventional motor insurance through its affiliated insurance entities. S&P Global Market Intelligence reported $1.036 billion of Tesla-affiliated direct written premiums in 2025, versus $1.37 billion for the wider Tesla insurance program including outside carriers. Gross written premiums are not identical to consolidated earned revenue. Tesla also originates certain vehicle and energy-product financing receivables, making the contract type and interest revenue relevant.

Corporate objective check: The Texas certificate of formation states a broad lawful-business purpose and does not explicitly name dealing in interest as a corporate objective, but that is insufficient to treat all affiliated insurance and customer-financing transactions as permissible. Qualified Shariah interpretation of incidental underwritten insurance remains material.

Primary evidence: Tesla September 2026 Form 10-Q; Texas certificate of formation; S&amp;P Global insurance underwriting research (12 May 2026)

E. Tradability / asset composition

AAOIFI SS21 3/19 addresses mixed-asset companies and a minimum 30% market value of tangible assets, benefits and rights relative to total asset value. Tesla reports $157.517B total assets, including property, plant and equipment $50.591B and inventory $14.092B. Their combined book carrying values total $64.683B, or 41.064% of reported assets, without even counting automotive lease vehicles ($4.047B) and energy systems ($4.453B).

The percentage strongly supports a substantive physical-asset business, but this is a book-value proxy, not a verified market-value appraisal. For that reason its result is INDICATIVE PASS, NOT FINAL VERIFICATION. No assumption is made that intangible accounting balances equal true market-valued rights.

G. Data quality and unresolved issues

The underlying financial report is the latest verified interim unaudited 10-Q for September 30, filed October 22. Tesla's 2025 audited 10-K is the previous audited anchor. Conventional debt and investment security-type classifications are directly disclosed, and observed market prices are dated. Primary evidence is high for these inputs.

  • Group reporting does not disaggregate insurance earned premiums, interest on customer-financing receivables or the Shariah form of each financing arrangement sufficiently for the complete 5% income test.
  • Conventional insurance directly written by Tesla subsidiaries is a real activity; whether its ancillary nature qualifies under SS21 needs qualified interpretation, rather than an inference that all manufacturing-adjacent activities are permitted.
  • AAOIFI 3/19 uses market value for the tradability test; we have a strong book-value proxy, not independently verified market valuation.
  • The AAOIFI portal continues to list SS21. The accessible public standards text does not establish the absence of all newer paid-edition commentary or Shariah board rulings.

Historical S&P insurance figures are independently reported but are not the same reporting period or measurement basis as Tesla's 2026 nine-month consolidated statement. Consequently they are not simply added to $1.301B to claim a final ratio.

H. AAOIFI guidance

Overall assessment: SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED. The low 0.66% conventional borrowing and 0.87% interest-taking deposit ratios are favorable, while the interest-only 1.63% figure is below the 5% cap. Nevertheless, AAOIFI equity eligibility depends on both the primary/peripheral business classification and all prohibited income, not just easily reported investment interest.

Do not call TSLA formally AAOIFI-certified or unconditionally compliant based on these financial ratios alone. To upgrade to AAOIFI-COMPLIANT, obtain a robust reconciliation of conventional insurance underwriting and financing revenue, confirmation of permitted/impermissible classification, and an adequate mixed-asset tradability basis. This is a Shariah classification, not a stock BUY/SELL recommendation.
