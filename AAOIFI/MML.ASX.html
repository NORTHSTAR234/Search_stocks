Executive summary

McLaren Minerals' primary activity is mineral exploration and advancement of its titanium/mineral-sands projects, which is not intrinsically prohibited. The audited FY2026 accounts disclose no interest-bearing borrowings, so the borrowing ratio is 0%, and disclose A$968,928 of interest-bearing cash, producing an estimated 17.43% deposit ratio using the reporting-date market-capitalisation convention; both are within the AAOIFI 30% limits. However, the company was pre-revenue and reported only A$12,696 of total income, all of which was interest income. The prohibited-income ratio is therefore 100.00%, far above the AAOIFI 5% limit, making the shares AAOIFI-NON-COMPLIANT on this screen. The indicative tradability/asset-composition calculation is about 62.10% using exploration/evaluation rights plus plant and equipment, but that classification is interpretive and does not affect the overall fail.

A. Business activity

McLaren Minerals Limited is an Australian exploration company focused on the development of the McLaren Titanium Project and related mineral-sands/silica interests. Mineral exploration and project development are not intrinsically prohibited activities under the AAOIFI business screen. The FY2026 company remained in the exploration/project-study phase and had no operating revenue. No prohibited operating segment was identified.

Corporate-objective evidence gap: public operational disclosures were reviewed, but a constitutional/objectives document explicitly addressing whether interest-based dealings are a corporate objective was not separately verified. This gap does not change the overall verdict because the prohibited-income ratio independently fails by a very large margin.

D. Market-capitalisation convention

AAOIFI implementation convention — reporting-date market cap. The screen uses 505,461,159 issued shares at 30 June 2026 and an observed June 2026 close of A$0.011 per share, giving an estimated market capitalisation of A$5,560,072.75. This is not a 24- or 36-month average. A current-price sensitivity around A$0.011 in November 2026 gives essentially the same ratio when the FY2026 numerator is held constant. The interest-taking deposit ratio would only breach 30% if the denominator fell below about A$3.23m, equivalent to roughly A$0.00639 per share on the FY2026 share count; the decisive prohibited-income failure is independent of market capitalisation.

E. Tradability / asset composition

The indicative numerator is A$4,639,117 of mineral exploration/evaluation assets plus A$7,692 of plant and equipment, or A$4,646,809. Against A$7,483,132 of total assets, this is approximately 62.10%, above the stored SS21 implementation threshold of 30%. Evidence quality is Medium because treating exploration/evaluation assets as AAOIFI-recognised benefits/rights is an interpretive classification and the accounts provide book values rather than a separate market-value assessment. This issue is not verdict-changing because the prohibited-income test already fails.

G. Data quality and unresolved issues

  • The decisive income figures, cash classification, total assets and share count come from the audited FY2026 financial statements and are high-quality inputs.
  • The reporting-date share price is based on market-history data rather than an issuer financial statement; this affects only the market-cap denominator and not the prohibited-income failure.
  • A public constitutional/objectives document was not separately verified.
  • The tradability calculation depends on treating capitalised exploration/evaluation assets as benefits/rights and uses book values; a qualified Shari'ah board may classify or value these differently.
  • The latest AAOIFI website check on 12 November 2026 still lists SS21, while a separate Shari'ah Standard on Purification of Impermissible Income remains under development.

H. AAOIFI guidance

Under this AAOIFI-first rules-based screen, MML.ASX is AAOIFI-NON-COMPLIANT for the FY2026 evidence set because prohibited income equals 100% of total income versus the 5% maximum.
