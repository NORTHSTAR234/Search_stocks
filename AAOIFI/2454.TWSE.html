Executive summary

MediaTek's semiconductor business appears permissible under AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21. Its borrowing and deposit exposures are comfortably below the 30% limits even using conservative accounting assumptions. However, the prohibited-income calculation is incomplete, the asset-composition test depends on qualifying intangible rights, and the treatment of a substantial zero-coupon convertible bond issue needs review. Therefore, the shares cannot yet be classified as AAOIFI-compliant.

A. Business activity

MediaTek designs, develops, manufactures via partners and markets integrated semiconductor circuits and related software/technical services, primarily for communication, consumer devices and emerging AI infrastructure. This is a generally permissible operating business. TWSE-listed business scope corroborates semiconductor activities. No prohibited primary operating segment was evidenced; full articles/memorandum and every subsidiary objective were not independently verified.

Controlling methodology: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21, Financial Paper (Shares and Bonds), still in AAOIFI issued-standards list checked November 2026. No superseding controlling standard identified, but full eStandards text could not be independently read. Stored SS21 methodology: conventional loans/market cap ≤30%, interest-taking deposits/market cap ≤30%, prohibited income/total income ≤5%, mixed-asset tradability test at least 30% qualifying tangible assets, benefits and rights.

D. Market-capitalisation convention

AAOIFI implementation convention, not asserted standard text: match the 30 September 2026 balance sheet to the TWSE September 30 closing price of NT$4,920 and the approximately 1,604,952,613 issued shares implied by reported share capital NT$16,049,526,130 divided by par NT$10. Indicative market capitalization = NT$7,896.37bn. Outstanding-share and treasury-share adjustments have not been reconciled, so this is an issued-share proxy, not definitive exact market cap.

Market-date sensitivity: November 11, 2026 closing price NT$3,990 gives indicative market cap NT$6,403.76bn on the same share proxy. Under this lower denominator, the broad total-liabilities borrowing envelope becomes 3.01% and broad cash/current-financial-assets-plus-investments envelope 7.19%; neither is near the 30% threshold. No 24- or 36-month averaging methodology was substituted for AAOIFI.

E. Tradability / asset composition

Book-value approximation from September 30 2026: inventories NT$153.531bn + PP&E NT$74.841bn = NT$228.372bn = 23.63% of NT$966.505bn total assets. Adding all NT$101.793bn of disclosed intangible assets would give 34.16%. The financial statement does not identify what proportion of the intangible balance constitutes eligible rights under SS21 or provide independently assessed market value of qualifying rights. Consequently this tradability test could switch from failing to passing solely on rights classification; no unconditional pass is issued.

F. Purification

Not calculable from reliable nine-month prohibited-income data. Under the stored SS21 interpretation, purification per share equals total prohibited income of the relevant reporting period divided by relevant shares outstanding. Investor amount equals per-share amount multiplied by investor shares held at period end. No number of user-held shares was supplied.

The 2026 first-half interest-income disclosure of NT$4.958682bn cannot stand in for nine-month 2026 prohibited income, which additionally requires a breakdown of Q3 income, prohibited investment gains and other items. The number of period-end outstanding shares needs verification against treasury stock, and no arbitrary dividend-based purification method was used.

G. Data quality and unresolved issues

  1. Disaggregate all interest-taking deposits and conventional financial assets; note that even intentionally broad sums remain below the 30% limit.
  2. Obtain gross Jan–Sep 2026 conventional interest income, investments and other prohibited revenue, not the net non-operating line.
  3. Verify how the US$3.9bn zero-coupon conventional convertible instrument affects corporate-objective treatment under AAOIFI SS21; zero coupon is not the same as AAOIFI-compliant sukuk.
  4. Obtain a reliable intangible-rights breakdown and market value/eligibility basis for the 30% tradability threshold.
  5. Confirm the issued/outstanding share-count bridge, corporate objectives and definitive SS21 text. Unreviewed subsidiaries and holdings remain a gap.

H. AAOIFI guidance

SHARIAH REVIEW REQUIRED is a rules-based evidence result, neither AAOIFI certification nor a categorical halal or haram conclusion. The company's core activity appears permissible and conservative leverage/deposit stress tests are comfortably below limits. But insufficient verification of the prohibited-income ratio and material interpretive questions around tradable intangible rights and the convertible bond prevent the more affirmative AAOIFI-COMPLIANT result. Consult an appropriately qualified Shariah adviser and obtain the detailed financial notes before relying on the shares as AAOIFI-eligible. This analysis is not investment or trading advice.
