2026 chronology: other gains and derivative positions

The NT$334.022m “other gains” category comprises Q1 NT$126.038m (derived by subtracting Q2 from the H1 filing), Q2 NT$73.810m (2026 half-year Note 6(19)) and Q3 NT$134.174m (Q3 Note 6(19)). The 2025 January–September comparable is NT$371.777m. This confirms recurring reported amounts across three quarters, not their business source. A generic classification such as scrap, subsidies, compensation, dividends or insurance would be speculation without the general-ledger breakdown.

Note 6(2) explicitly identifies a USD10m forward sale of USD for TWD at 31 December 2025, maturing 12 January 2026, with a year-end derivative liability of NT$9.475m. The filing says no such derivative instruments were outstanding at 30 September 2026; it does not demonstrate zero derivative dealings throughout 2026. The January–September FVTPL gain is NT$9.475m and Q3 FVTPL gain is zero. Exact cash settlement mechanics and compliance with Islamic currency-trading/hedging rules are not disclosed.

The issuer's Kunshan relocation agreement covers a total RMB195m compensation arrangement, but NT$375.092m received as of September 2026 is shown as advance receipts in liabilities pending conveyance. It is not support for claiming the recognized NT$334.022m “other gains” are relocation income. The filing also discusses a historical fire-insurance receivable; this is not proof of income in the 2026 other-gains line. Foreign-operation currency translation gains of NT$2,821.920m are presented in other comprehensive income (OCI), not the period's income statement, and are not automatically added as current gross prohibited income.

G. Data quality and unresolved issues

  • The primary source is the issuer's CPA-reviewed Q3 2026 consolidated report (published around 28–29 October 2026). It is the latest confirmed financial position for this screening; the audited annual report is a background cross-check.
  • IBKR supplied USD-converted Q3 summary and LSEG investment/financial panels, not the primary regulatory source. Q3 annual-style and TTM dollar numbers must not be mixed with nine-month TWD numerators without a consistent timeframe and currency.
  • The gross prohibited-income ratio requires classification of the NT$334.022m “other gains” component, FVTPL underlying instruments, and any gross/net offsets. The visible 0.450% calculation is diagnostic, not proof of a full maximum.
  • Interest-taking deposit breakdown lacks contract-level characterization. The extreme all-assets ceiling nevertheless robustly passes the financial 30% deposit limit at either relevant price.
  • Corporate constitutional objective clauses have not been independently subjected to a formal SS21 review.
  • Recent share-price declines alter percentage denominators but are not evidence that a manufacturing activity became prohibited.

Exactly what remains to be verified

  1. Other gains NT$334.022m: ask issuer finance/IR for line-by-line nature, payer, gross income, offsets and whether bank/insurance/government subsidies, penalties, settlements, scrap or service income are included.
  2. FX net gains NT$91.271m: identify gross realized and unrealized gains/losses and whether amounts arise from export receivables, import payables, bank deposits, loans, ordinary spot settlement or forwards.
  3. FVTPL NT$9.475m: obtain the January 2026 USD10m forward settlement statement, its purpose and complete payoff. Evaluate Islamic currency-trading/hedging principles; no open derivative at 30 September is not equivalent to never having transacted one.
  4. Complete prohibited income: reconcile bank-deposit interest and gains from each subsidiary, security, financing/deposit instrument and other financial transaction to a gross, not net, numerator; verify correct total-income denominator and year-to-date period.
  5. Corporate objective: complete SS21 memorandum review; do not infer a prohibited corporate purpose merely from incidental financing.

Bottom line: identified interest is conclusively a prohibited-income component; FX is not automatically prohibited; the small FVTPL gain deserves a contract review; and NT$334.022m other gains are not explained by published notes. The mathematical screen looks favourable, but a final AAOIFI-COMPLIANT status is not warranted yet.

H. AAOIFI guidance

Under the available evidence, the operating business and the borrowing, deposit and asset-composition ratio tests present no disqualifying result. The correct conservative screen is INSUFFICIENT DATA. The reported positive net non-operating components produce a reassuring hypothetical 0.4499% of operating revenue if all were prohibited; however gross classification, the forward-FX payoff, other gains and corporate objectives are unresolved and prevent certifying the actual 5% rule. This is not a finding of a prohibited core business or a verified financial-ratio failure. Do not treat the broker's negative net interest line as eliminating interest-income purification.
